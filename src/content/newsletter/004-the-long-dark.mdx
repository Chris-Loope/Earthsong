*December Solstice · December 21, 2025 · 15:03 UTC*

At the December solstice, the Sun reaches its southernmost position in Earth's sky. The Northern Hemisphere receives its shortest span of daylight and begins astronomical winter; the Southern Hemisphere receives its longest daylight and begins summer.

The word *solstice* comes from roots meaning that the Sun stands still. For several days, the position of sunrise or sunset changes so little that the Sun can appear to pause at the edge of its yearly journey before reversing direction.

## In the past: holding a place for return

Ancient structures preserve close observation of this turning. At Newgrange in Ireland, the rising winter-solstice Sun enters a passage and illuminates an inner chamber. At Stonehenge in England, the monument's principal axis frames midsummer sunrise in one direction and midwinter sunset in the other.

We cannot recover every belief held by the people who built these places. We can see the work required to mark a recurring event beyond one person's lifetime. Their attention was not casual. They made knowledge of return durable.

## In the present: the darkest day has uneven edges

The solstice brings the least total daylight in the Northern Hemisphere, but it is usually neither the latest sunrise nor the earliest sunset. Near 40 degrees north latitude, the earliest sunset often comes in early December and the latest sunrise in early January. Earth's tilted axis, elliptical orbit, and the difference between clock time and solar time separate those dates.

This is a useful correction to a familiar story. Change rarely arrives everywhere at once. One measure may have turned while another still appears to worsen. The total daylight begins to grow after the solstice even while mornings may continue getting darker for a time.

## For the future: let rest prepare something

Rest is not the opposite of useful action. Sleep consolidates learning. Fallow time can reveal which effort is necessary and which is only motion. Maintenance prevents replacement. Quiet can return attention to details that speed concealed.

The future does not need every moment of the present to be productive. It does benefit when rest leaves a person more able to notice, choose, and care.

## A practice for one person

At or after sunset, spend ten minutes with only the light necessary for safety. Put away the screen. Let your eyes adjust. Notice which sounds become clearer and which parts of the room or landscape disappear.

Then choose one thing not to carry into the next season: an unnecessary purchase, a recurring distraction, an object that can be repaired or passed on, or an obligation that no longer serves its purpose. Write down the decision before acting on it.

## Carry forward

The return of light is real, but it is gradual. A positive direction does not need to look dramatic on its first day.
