## Compassion and Repair

Compassion is the discipline of taking another life seriously. It begins with attention, but it cannot end with a feeling. It asks what care is wanted, what harm is occurring, what responsibility is ours, and what repair is possible.

Earthsong does not describe the Earth as nurturing all life without discrimination. Living systems contain cooperation and competition, shelter and exposure, flourishing and loss. Nature can inform us without serving as a simple moral example. Compassion is a human and communal choice made within that complexity.

### Four movements of compassion

- **Attention:** Listen before deciding what another person, community, animal, or place needs. Curiosity is more reliable than projection.
- **Consent:** Help offered without regard for agency can become control. Ask when possible; respect a refusal; do not treat access to another's story, body, time, or tradition as a reward for good intentions.
- **Boundaries:** Compassion does not require endless availability, restored closeness, or tolerance of harm. A boundary can protect the conditions in which future care and honesty are possible.
- **Repair:** When harm has occurred, name it accurately, stop repeating it, accept proportionate consequences, restore what can be restored, and change the conditions that allowed it. An apology may begin repair, but it does not complete it.

Forgiveness may be freely chosen. It is never owed, and it is not the measure of whether someone has healed.

### Compassion beyond the human

Care for more-than-human life starts with needs rather than sentiment. A wild animal may need distance, not touch. A stream may need less runoff, not an offering. A tree may need suitable soil and long-term protection, not a ribbon tied around its bark. Evidence, local knowledge, and skilled stewardship help translate affection into useful action.

Justice belongs inside compassion. If the same people or places repeatedly carry the cost of another's comfort, kindness at the surface is not enough. Care must also examine power, distribution, and whose voice can shape the decision.

### A practice of accountable care

Before one act of help, ask:

1. What do I know, and what am I assuming?
2. Who has named the need?
3. Is my action welcome and within my capacity?
4. What would still matter after the feeling of urgency passes?

Choose the smallest response that is both useful now and honest enough to continue. Compassion becomes part of the Earthsong when attention changes behaviour.
