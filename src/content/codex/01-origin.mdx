## The Origin Symphony

Earthsong begins with a creation metaphor.

Before there was a listener, there was silence. Then came the **First Note**: not a literal sound offered as cosmology, but an image for the first difference, the first relationship, the first moment from which another moment could follow. In Earthsong's telling, stars, stone, water, cells, and conscious life enter the composition one after another. No note makes the whole song alone.

This is a story about belonging. It is not a scientific account of how the universe began.

### Where the metaphor ends

Science describes an observable history: the universe expanded from an early hot, dense state; stars formed and made many of the elements later gathered into planets; Earth formed about 4.5 billion years ago; and life diversified through deep time. Those explanations rest on evidence and remain open to refinement. The First Note does not replace them or fill their unanswered questions with certainty.

Earthsong also uses phrases such as *the living Earth* and *the world sings*. These are relational language. They point to a planet shaped by living systems and to the exchanges that sustain them. They do not claim that a rock is biologically alive, that nature has one intention, or that a hidden vibration explains physical events.

Wonder is strongest when it does not ask metaphor to impersonate fact.

### What the story carries

The Origin Symphony offers three propositions for living:

- **Existence is relational.** Breath, food, water, shelter, climate, and community arise through systems no individual made alone.
- **Difference belongs in harmony.** Harmony is not sameness. It is the work of allowing distinct lives and needs to coexist without one voice consuming the rest.
- **Participation creates responsibility.** To belong to a living system is to affect it. Care begins by noticing what our choices require and where their consequences travel.

Earthsong does not ask anyone to believe that the universe was literally sung into being. It asks what might change if life were treated less like a collection of possessions and more like a composition we briefly join.

### A practice of origin

Choose one ordinary thing you used today: a glass of water, a piece of fruit, a sheet of paper, a charged device. Trace as much of its story as you honestly can. What materials made it? What living and human work brought it to you? Where will it go after use?

Where knowledge ends, write *unknown* rather than inventing an answer. The unknown is not an empty space to conquer. It is a reason to keep listening.
